# Intro

When desiging this blog, I wanted to add something a little folky and old maybe as a contrast to the overall academic style of the blog. I thought about many decorative images I could pull from obscure illlustrated books. I wanted something that would acknowldege the interdislpinary and maybe helter-skelter topics in the blog. However I eventually settled on line breaks where the distinctive form of the sacred harp notes could be exemplified. 

This matched both place, history, and theological themes of the blog and of course I had to implement it. While it could have been done before I could find no evidence of it online. My main project is the Crucified ground where I look at the history of Oak Ridge as a test of theology. The seven note system was published in Knoxville TN. WHile it is hard to say if the communities located around Oak Ridge even sang shape notes and whether they use the DoReME system or the FaSoLa system is even harder to say at this point. The haunting communal, audienceless nature of the Sacred Harp chorus is hard to dispute. The democratic pedagogy is also a really cool theme to link to. 

# A brief history of line breaks

WIth the advent of hypertext making webpages independent of screen size, it is often recommended to use a blank line space or a centered em dash with a flush-left paragraph to make sure the break is recognized in any screen output. Its almost like we are back during the printing press where the mechanics of the printing press required an object printed in the gaps both to make reassure the reader that no error in the printing occured and because The physical reality of lead and wood spacers meant that leaving a blank line required inserting physical, non-printing material into the forme. Space had to be locked into place.
