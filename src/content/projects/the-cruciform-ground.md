The Cruciform Ground is a place-based theological investigation arising from an effort to understand Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Through sustained engagement with environmental science, history, and Christian theological traditions, particularly the theology of the cross, it explores questions of creation, corruption, suffering, responsibility, and hope. The inquiry does not presume that the landscape independently reveals God or that its theological questions must receive consoling answers.

## Who was moved?

Begin with the land acquired for the Manhattan Project and the people who had to leave it. The inquiry also follows the segregation that shaped where Black workers and their families could live, and the history residents made in Scarboro.

## What was left in the ground and water?

The inquiry follows industrial releases through soils, streams, and sediments. It asks what the records can establish about where contaminants went, who encountered them, and what persists.

## What can theology say here?

Holy Saturday and the theology of the cross frame a question about what Christians can say in the presence of suffering and damage that remain unresolved.

## Selected sources

- Charles W. Johnson and Charles O. Jackson’s *City Behind a Fence* provides a starting point for the land acquisition and construction of wartime Oak Ridge.
- The [ATSDR public health assessment of Y-12 mercury releases](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/Y12ORRMercury/Y12ORRMercuryPHA33012.pdf) documents how investigators evaluated historical mercury releases and potential exposure pathways.

## Organizing dimensions

The inquiry follows **space** (the physical landscape and the ways it has been measured), **place** (the landscape as inhabited and remembered), and **time** (the different durations of geological, ecological, and human change).
