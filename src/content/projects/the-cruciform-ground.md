The Cruciform Ground is a place-based theological investigation arising from an effort to understand Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Through sustained engagement with environmental science, history, and Christian theological traditions, particularly the theology of the cross, it explores questions of creation, corruption, suffering, responsibility, and hope. The inquiry does not presume that the landscape independently reveals God or that its theological questions must receive consoling answers.

## Organizing dimensions

The inquiry is organized loosely around three dimensions, each of which asks something different of the evidence.

- **Space.** The physical landscape: its geology, soils, waters, and ecology, and the ways they have been measured and altered.
- **Place.** The landscape as inhabited and remembered: communities, institutions, displacements, and the meanings people have given to the ground.
- **Time.** The long and short durations at work in one location, from geological and ecological time to the decades of human history and the persistence of what humans leave behind.

These are working categories, open to revision as the research develops.
