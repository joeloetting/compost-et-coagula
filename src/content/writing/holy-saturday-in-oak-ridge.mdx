A landscape can be described through its geology, its industrial history, and the lives of those who inhabited it. Yet none of these descriptions alone settles the question of what happened in a place, and none of them, taken by itself, tells us what the place might mean. The temptation in writing about any landscape that carries a heavy history is to move too quickly from description to verdict: to read a ridge, a stream, or a fenced parcel of ground as though it were already a parable whose moral could be stated in a sentence. This essay, such as it is, is an exercise in resisting that temptation for as long as honesty requires.

To begin with the evidence is not to abandon interpretation. It is to acknowledge that interpretation carries obligations to the people, places, and histories it seeks to understand.[^evidence] The order of operations matters. A theological reading that arrives before the evidence will tend to find only what it brought, and a historical reading that refuses every question of meaning will tend to mistake its own reticence for neutrality. Neither posture is adequate to a place where the record is incomplete, where some of the people most affected left the fewest documents, and where the ground itself continues to change on timescales that do not match the length of any human argument.

## The day between

In the Christian calendar, Holy Saturday is the day between the crucifixion and the resurrection: a day in which, liturgically speaking, very little happens. The tomb is sealed. The disciples are scattered. The narrative pauses.

> So they went, and made the sepulchre sure, sealing the stone, and setting a watch.
>
> — Matthew 27:66 (King James Version)

The attraction of Holy Saturday as an interpretive frame is precisely its refusal of resolution.[^frame] It names a condition in which the worst has happened and the meaning of what happened is not yet available. Whether that condition is a fitting lens for a particular landscape is a question the essay must earn the right to ask; it cannot be assumed at the outset. The paragraph you are reading is long on purpose, so that the measure, leading, and paragraph spacing can be judged across the kind of sustained argument that scholarly prose often requires, where a single thought is qualified, extended, and qualified again before the paragraph is allowed to end, and where the reader needs the line length to remain comfortable even as the sentences accumulate clause upon clause.

Some essays will need to discuss quantities: lengths of time, measured values, or counts of documents. In this fixture they are deliberately absent, because inventing them would defeat the purpose of a publication that cares about evidence. Where an essay does report measurements, it should give their source, their units, and enough context for a reader to judge their reliability.
